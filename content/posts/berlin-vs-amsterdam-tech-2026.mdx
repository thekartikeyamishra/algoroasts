Two of Europe's most attractive tech hubs, two genuinely different bets. If you're a developer choosing between Berlin and Amsterdam in 2026, the decision comes down to scale and cost versus pay and lifestyle.

This is general information, not legal, tax, or financial advice. Confirm tax rules and immigration specifics with qualified professionals.

## Ecosystem and roles

Berlin has the larger and more varied scene: a dense startup and scale-up base plus international employers, with a famously English-friendly working culture — covered in the broader [Germany market guide](/blog/germany-software-engineer-salary-2026). Amsterdam is smaller but punches above its weight, hosting fintech, scale-ups, and European HQs of global firms, with English widely used in tech.

## Pay and tax

Amsterdam tends to offer higher nominal salaries, and the Netherlands' 30% ruling can give qualifying newcomers a meaningful tax advantage for a period (rules have tightened — verify current terms). Berlin's pay is solid but lower, offset by lower living costs. For contractors, both markets reward scarce skills — see the [EU day-rate guide](/blog/eu-contractor-day-rates-2026).

## Cost of living and housing

This is the decisive practical factor. Both cities have housing shortages, but Amsterdam's is more severe and expensive; Berlin remains comparatively affordable despite rising rents. Factor real housing access into any offer — a higher Amsterdam salary can be eroded by housing costs and scarcity.

## Relocation

Both are reachable for non-EU developers via the [EU Blue Card](/blog/eu-blue-card-tech-relocation-2026), with thresholds set nationally between 1.0 and 1.6x the average gross salary per the [European Commission](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/legal-migration-and-resettlement/work/eu-blue-card-attracting-highly-qualified-talent-eu_en). If you're also weighing other continents, the [Canada vs USA comparison](/blog/canada-vs-usa-tech-careers-2026) applies the same decision logic.

## The directive

Choose Berlin for a larger, more affordable ecosystem and an easier soft landing; choose Amsterdam for higher pay, possible tax advantages, and quality of life — provided you can secure housing. Verify tax and immigration specifics before deciding.
