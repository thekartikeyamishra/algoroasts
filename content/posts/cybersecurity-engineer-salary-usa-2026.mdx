## What the data says

Per the [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/information-security-analysts.htm), the median annual wage for information security analysts was <Stat value="$124,910" label="US median, 2024" /> in May 2024, with the top 10% earning over $186,420. Employment is projected to grow <Stat value="29%" label="2024–2034" /> over the decade — far above the 3% average for all occupations — adding roughly 16,000 openings a year. Finance, insurance, and information sectors pay among the highest medians.

## Why the shortage persists

Attack surfaces expand faster than teams can hire. Every new cloud deployment, AI system, and connected device adds risk, while the supply of qualified defenders lags. The [Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency](https://www.cisa.gov/) and industry workforce studies consistently describe chronic understaffing. For a developer, that gap is leverage: demand this durable rarely softens.

## The highest-paying specializations

Generalist security roles pay well; specialists pay best. Cloud security (securing AWS, Azure, and GCP workloads), application and product security, and AI-driven detection command the top bands — the same cloud-and-security combination our [cloud certifications analysis](/blog/cloud-certifications-worth-it-2026) flags as a goldmine pairing. Security depth also accelerates leveling in big tech, as the [FAANG compensation breakdown](/blog/faang-software-engineer-compensation-2026) shows.

## The directive: what to build

Pick a lane and go deep. For most developers, the highest-ROI path is cloud security: learn one cloud's security model thoroughly, earn a recognized security credential, and prove it by hardening and monitoring a real deployment. Pair that with privacy fundamentals — increasingly inseparable from security, as our [privacy-engineering guide](/blog/gdpr-privacy-engineering-2026) explains — and you are credible for roles at the top of the range.
