The most common mistake when reading a big-tech offer is anchoring on base salary. At FAANG-tier companies the base is the smallest interesting variable. Level and equity are where the money is — and where your negotiating leverage actually lives.

## The three components

Every big-tech package stacks three parts. **Base salary** is steady cash. **Equity**, usually restricted stock units vesting over several years, is the component that scales with level and with company stock performance. **Bonus** is a target percentage of base. The headline number recruiters quote blends all three, often averaged over the vesting period — which is why two offers with identical bases can differ enormously.

For grounding, the [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/software-developers.htm) reports a median wage near $105,990 for software developers, QA analysts, and testers in May 2024. Big-tech senior total comp sits well above that once equity is counted — and the spread is the story.

## Why level dominates

Inside these companies, pay is bracketed by level (the internal ladder), not by the words on your business card. Moving up one level can change total comp more than years of tenure at the same level. That makes leveling — at the offer stage and in promotion cycles — the single highest-leverage thing to optimize.

## What moves you up the ladder

The same scarce skills that lift pay across the industry accelerate leveling here. As our [AI engineer salary analysis](/blog/ai-engineer-salary-2026) documents, the market is paying for architecture, [AI orchestration](/blog/ml-engineer-vs-data-scientist-2026), and complex problem-solving — not boilerplate. Security depth is its own premium track, as the [cybersecurity salary data](/blog/cybersecurity-engineer-salary-usa-2026) shows. Demonstrated ownership of hard systems is what promotion committees reward.

## The directive

Negotiate the level, not just the base. Understand the vesting schedule and refresh policy before you sign. And invest your learning in the scarce, hard-to-automate skills that move you up brackets — because at this tier, one level of progression compounds across base, equity, and bonus at once.
