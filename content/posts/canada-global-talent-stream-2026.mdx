If Express Entry is the marathon to Canadian PR, the Global Talent Stream is the sprint to a Canadian job. For developers with an offer, it's the fastest legal route into the country — often in about two weeks.

This is general information, not legal or immigration advice. Confirm details with IRCC/ESDC and a regulated professional.

## What the GTS is

The Global Talent Stream, part of Canada's Global Skills Strategy, lets employers hire highly skilled foreign workers fast. Its hallmark is the two-week processing target — per the [Government of Canada](https://www.canada.ca/tech-talent-immigration), the 2025 median was about 12 days, and the program has issued over 58,000 permits since 2017. For a developer, that speed is transformative compared with most immigration timelines.

## The two categories

**Category A** is for high-growth companies referred by a designated partner to hire unique, specialized talent. **Category B** covers employers hiring for one of 22 in-demand occupations on the government list — including software engineers, cybersecurity analysts, and related roles — without needing a referral. Most developers enter via Category B.

## How it fits a path to PR

The GTS is a work permit, not permanent residence — but it's a powerful on-ramp. Canadian work experience gained through it materially strengthens an [Express Entry](/blog/canada-express-entry-tech-2026) profile, which matters more now that STEM draws are paused. Tech hubs Toronto, Montreal, Vancouver, and Calgary lean heavily on the program, and it's a common route into Canada's [AI job market](/blog/ai-jobs-canada-2026). Compared with the [US H-1B lottery](/blog/tech-work-visa-options-usa-2026), the GTS is faster and more predictable.

## The directive

Secure a job offer with a GTS-eligible employer — most developers qualify under Category B's occupation list — to enter Canada in roughly two weeks, then use the Canadian experience to build toward permanent residence. Verify eligibility and current occupation lists with IRCC/ESDC.
