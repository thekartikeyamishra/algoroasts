If you want one trustworthy number for what software developers earn in Canada, use the Government of Canada's **Job Bank**. It publishes low, median and high hourly wages for the occupation "software developers and programmers" (NOC 21232) for every province, and it is the source immigration and labour-market programmes refer to.

## Job Bank wages by province (NOC 21232)

Hourly figures are Job Bank's published low, median and high wages (updated November 19, 2025). The annual column is our own arithmetic — median × 2,080 hours, rounded — to make comparison with salary offers easier.

Job Bank does not publish figures for the three territories for this occupation. Wages also vary within provinces: the Windsor-Sarnia region of Ontario, for example, has a median of CAD 43.27/hour, below the provincial figure. Check your city on Job Bank before you negotiate.

## How to read these numbers

- **Low, median, high are not junior, mid, senior.** They describe the spread of wages actually paid. Experienced engineers at large employers often sit at or above the "high" figure.
- **They are base wages.** Bonuses, equity and benefits come on top. Job Bank reports that 94.8% of workers in this occupation nationally (93.6% in Ontario) receive at least one non-wage benefit, such as a pension plan, health insurance or paid leave.
- **They lag the market.** Official wages are survey-based and updated periodically, so treat them as a floor for negotiation, not a ceiling.

## City choice: Toronto, Vancouver and the rest

British Columbia's higher median reflects Vancouver's tech cluster, while Ontario's is anchored by Toronto, Ottawa and Waterloo. Lower-cost cities such as Calgary or Montreal can leave you better off after housing costs even at a lower wage. The trade-offs are covered in our [Toronto vs Vancouver comparison](/blog/toronto-vs-vancouver-tech-2026).

## The US gap — and how to close it

Canadian developer pay is materially lower than comparable US roles once converted to USD. The [Canada vs USA comparison](/blog/canada-vs-usa-tech-careers-2026) weighs that against healthcare, cost of living and immigration. The most direct way to close the gap is remote work for a US employer — and if that offer is a contract rather than a job, run it through the [Contract vs Full-Time Calculator](/tools/contract-vs-full-time-calculator) with the Canada preset, which accounts for self-employed CPP contributions.

## The directive

Find your province's median in the table, then position yourself above it: specialise in a scarce area (cloud, AI, security), negotiate with the "high" figure as your anchor if you're experienced, and compare total compensation, not just base pay.
