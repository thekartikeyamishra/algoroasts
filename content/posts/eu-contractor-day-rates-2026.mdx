Contracting in the EU pays well if you price correctly — and badly if you anchor to the wrong number. The most common mistake is converting an old salary directly into a rate. Here is how to do it properly.

This is general guidance, not financial or tax advice. Rates, taxes, and contracting structures vary by country — consult a local accountant.

## What drives an EU day rate

Three factors dominate. **Skill scarcity** — cloud, AI, and security command the highest rates, mirroring the [permanent-market premiums](https://www.skillsoft.com/blog/top-paying-aws-certifications) Skillsoft documents. **Country and client** — rates in high-cost markets and at enterprises or finance clients run far above startup or lower-cost-country rates. **Specialization** — a recognized expert in a niche prices well above a generalist.

## Converting a target into a rate

Do not divide salary by working days. A contractor carries costs an employee does not: no paid holiday, no benefits, gaps between contracts, their own tooling, taxes, and admin. A defensible rate starts from your annual income target, adds those costs, and divides by realistic billable days (well under 220). The result is meaningfully higher than a naive salary conversion — as it should be.

## Benchmarks and leverage

Anchor to the market, not your history. The [US contractor rate guide](/blog/software-contractor-rates-usa-2026) gives a high-end reference point, and developers contracting across borders should read the [USD remote-work playbook](/blog/remote-developer-jobs-india-usd-2026). Where you are based matters too — the cost and demand differences between hubs are real, as the [Berlin vs Amsterdam comparison](/blog/berlin-vs-amsterdam-tech-2026) shows, and the [Germany salary guide](/blog/germany-software-engineer-salary-2026) gives a permanent-market baseline to price above.

## The directive

Price against the value you deliver and the local market, convert your income target into a rate that accounts for all non-billable costs, and lead with a scarce specialization. The contractor who prices on value, not history, earns multiples more for the same skill.
