Going independent can pay far more than a salary — but only if you price like a business, not an employee. The most common and costly mistake US contractors make is anchoring their rate to their old paycheck.

This is general guidance, not financial or tax advice. Consult a CPA for your situation.

## Why contractor rates exceed salaries

An employee's salary hides a lot: employer-paid taxes, health insurance, paid leave, retirement match, equipment, and the guarantee of continuous pay. A contractor pays for all of that themselves and absorbs the gaps between engagements. So a contractor rate that merely matches a salary's hourly equivalent is a pay cut. The [BLS median of ~$105,990](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/software-developers.htm) for developers is a salaried floor to price well above.

## Calculating a defensible rate

Work backward from a fully-loaded annual income target — the take-home you want, plus self-employment taxes, health insurance, retirement, equipment, software, and a buffer for downtime. Then divide by realistic billable hours, which are far fewer than 2,080: sales, admin, and gaps cut deeply into billable time. The honest divisor produces a rate that often surprises people — and it should.

## Worked out: the 1099 tax you can't ignore

As a 1099 contractor you pay self-employment tax of 15.3% (12.4% Social Security plus 2.9% Medicare), according to the [IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/self-employment-tax-social-security-and-medicare-taxes). An employee has only half of that, 7.65%, withheld, because the employer pays the other half. That 7.65-point difference comes straight out of your rate, before benefits, unpaid holidays or gaps between contracts.

A W-2 contract through an agency is different (the agency pays the employer share), so there's a separate preset for it.

## What raises your rate

Scarcity and positioning. Cloud, AI, and security specialists price at the top, just as they top the [salaried premium tables](/blog/faang-software-engineer-compensation-2026). A recognized expert in a specific niche commands far more than a generalist. And value-based pricing — charging for the outcome you deliver, not the hours you log — is the highest tier of all.

## Market context

Benchmark against the market, not your history. US rates set the global high-water mark — the [EU day-rate guide](/blog/eu-contractor-day-rates-2026) and the [USD remote-work playbook](/blog/remote-developer-jobs-india-usd-2026) show how this reference travels internationally. If you're weighing contracting against an equity-heavy startup offer, understand the [equity math](/blog/startup-equity-explained-usa-2026) first — they're different risk profiles.

## The directive

Price like a business: build a fully-loaded income target, divide by honest billable hours, benchmark to the market, and lead with a scarce specialization or value-based pricing. The contractor who prices correctly earns well above the salaried equivalent for the same skill.
