## What the Blue Card is

The EU Blue Card grants highly qualified non-EU workers the right to live and work in an EU member state, with a path toward long-term residence and improved intra-EU mobility. Per the [European Commission](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/migration-and-asylum/legal-migration-and-resettlement/work/eu-blue-card-attracting-highly-qualified-talent-eu_en), the revised Directive (member states transposed it by late 2023) deliberately lowered barriers.

## The 2026 reforms that matter for developers

Three changes help tech workers directly. Thresholds dropped to a range of 1.0–1.6x the national average gross salary. The minimum contract length fell to six months. And critically, IT, cybersecurity, and AI professionals with roughly three years of relevant experience can now qualify without a university degree in many countries.

## Thresholds vary by country

The salary bar is set nationally, so it differs widely. As directional 2025–2026 figures: Germany's general threshold sits around the high-€40,000s with a lower shortage-occupation bar for IT; other states publish their own. Germany's strong tech market makes it a common destination — see the [Germany software engineer salary guide](/blog/germany-software-engineer-salary-2026). Always verify the current number for your target country.

## How it compares to other routes

Weigh the Blue Card against alternatives. The [US visa routes](/blog/tech-work-visa-options-usa-2026) face the H-1B lottery; [Canada's pathways](/blog/canada-express-entry-tech-2026) are often faster and more predictable. And if relocation is uncertain, [USD remote work](/blog/remote-developer-jobs-india-usd-2026) captures global pay without moving at all.

## The directive

Identify your target EU country, confirm its current Blue Card salary threshold and whether your experience qualifies you without a degree, and apply with a qualifying offer of six months or more. Verify every detail with official sources and qualified counsel.
